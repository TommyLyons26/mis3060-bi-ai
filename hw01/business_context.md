## My Prompt
Answer the following strategic question: When underwriting or monitoring a loan, credit score, debt-to-income ratio, and annual income each tell you something different about a borrower. What does each of these actually capture that the others don't, and where could relying too heavily on just one of them mislead a lender about a borrower's real risk?

## Claudes Summarized Response
 Credit score captures a borrower's historical repayment behaviour, debt-to-income (DTI) captures their current debt relative to income, and annual income captures their earnings capacity. None of the three tells you the whole story alone. A high credit score can mask debt just taken on that has not hit the report yet. A comfortable DTI is only a snapshot and says nothing about payment discipline or income stability. A high income can hide someone who's already overextended or has a poor repayment history. The real risk is treating any of these as a stand-in for the others, since each one catches a different type of risky borrower that the others would miss.
